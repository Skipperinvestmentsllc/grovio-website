# grovio Guide Publishing Plan

## Purpose

The grovio Guide is the public, free editorial library for homeschool parents.
It is not an in-app feature and it is not a high-volume SEO blog. Each article
should answer one real question with Claire's warm, practical voice while making
the reader feel steadier in the homeschool they are already building.

The app is the companion for keeping attendance, rhythm, and portfolio records.
The Guide earns trust, answers questions, and naturally points readers to the
right next step when grovio can help.

## Content Pillars

1. Getting started and confidence: questions such as whether a parent is doing
   enough, where to begin, and how to make choices without copying another
   family.
2. Records and state expectations: attendance, portfolios, reports, notices,
   and the state-records pilot. Compliance articles must state their review date,
   cite primary state sources, and explain that they are guidance rather than
   legal advice.
3. Daily rhythm and planning: flexible routines, multiple ages, curriculum
   choices, time, and consistency without burnout.
4. Learning in real life: what counts as learning, motivation, resistance,
   progress, and child-led learning.
5. Hard and tender parts: overwhelm, comparison, relationship tension, and
   confidence. These should meet the emotional question before offering tools.
6. Keeping the proof: portfolio moments, attendance, end-of-year reflection,
   and records. Mention grovio only where it is genuinely useful.

## Article Standard

Every new article should include:

- A question-led H1 that matches the language a parent would actually search.
- A short direct answer immediately below the introduction, in Claire's voice.
- A human byline that links to Claire's page and an accurate `datePublished`.
- A `dateModified` only when there was a real editorial or factual update.
- A plain-language state or legal disclaimer whenever requirements are discussed.
- Two or three related Guide links chosen for the reader's next question.
- One relevant grovio link only when the app is a natural next step, with no
  feature dump or forced sales language.
- Descriptive title, meta description, canonical URL, Article schema, and a
  featured image only when it adds meaning rather than repeating existing art.

## URL and Metadata Convention

- Publish at `/guide/{plain-kebab-case-question}.html`.
- Use the clean canonical URL `https://grovioapp.com/guide/{slug}`.
- Keep URLs stable after publishing. Improve the page in place rather than
  creating near-duplicate versions for small wording changes.
- Add the public canonical URL to `sitemap.xml` when the article is published.
- Use a unique meta title and description that state the question and the value
  of the answer, not a string of keywords.

## Editorial Workflow

1. Choose one reader question from search demand, support questions, podcast
   themes, or the Guide's related-question gaps.
2. Draft the quick answer and article outline before writing the full piece.
3. Verify every factual claim. For state requirements, use primary government or
   statutory sources and capture the source URL and review date in the draft.
4. Edit for Claire's voice: warm, clear, specific, and never alarmist or
   preachy.
5. Build the page from the existing Guide article template, add its metadata and
   schema, and add it to the Guide hub under one primary path.
6. Add related links both from the new article and from the most relevant older
   articles. Do not add every article everywhere.
7. Check mobile layout, internal links, sitemap entry, and structured data before
   publishing.
8. After publishing, link the episode page or podcast show notes when the topic
   is a natural companion; do not duplicate the transcript as an article.

## Cadence and Quality Bar

Start with one well-researched article per week. A useful update to an existing
article is often better than a thin new post. Publish only when the question is
distinct, the answer is specific, and the page helps a real parent make a calmer
next decision.

Avoid mass keyword pages, cloned state pages, invented freshness dates, or
generic AI summaries. The Guide should become easier to trust as it grows.

## State-Records Pilot

The homeschool-records-by-state tool is its own product-quality project. It
should remain a featured Guide tool, not a template for automatically generated
state articles. Expand it state by state only after source review, legal
disclaimer review, and an accurate "reviewed as of" date are complete.
