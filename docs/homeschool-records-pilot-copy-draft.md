## Global Disclaimer

This is general homeschool recordkeeping guidance, not legal advice. Requirements can change, and your official state source, district, evaluator, or association gets the final word. We reviewed these notes on July 29, 2026, and included source links so you can double-check before you rely on anything.

For families just beginning, the first question is often not "what records do I keep?" It is "how do I officially start?" Each state section should include a first-step note about notice of intent, district filing, association/enrollment route, and formal school withdrawal where that question is relevant.

## South Carolina

Reviewed on: July 29, 2026

Tier: Multi-pathway state

### Quick Answer

South Carolina has three homeschool options, so the right recordkeeping answer depends on the path you are using. Do not try to memorize all three. Start by finding your option, then your record list gets much clearer.

Official places to verify:

- South Carolina Department of Education: https://www.ed.sc.gov/districts-schools/state-accountability/home-schooling/
- Option 1 statute, SC Code Section 59-65-40: https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/2024/title-59/chapter-65/section-59-65-40/
- Option 2 statute, SC Code Section 59-65-45: https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-59/chapter-65/section-59-65-45/
- Option 3 statute, SC Code Section 59-65-47: https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-59/chapter-65/section-59-65-47/

### Option 1: School District

Option 1 is the more district-involved path. Your homeschool program is approved through the local district board of trustees, and the statute includes more direct district-facing requirements.

What to consider keeping:

- District approval/application records.
- 180-day attendance record.
- Hours record showing the instructional-day requirement.
- Required-subject plan.
- Plan book, diary, or written record of subjects and activities.
- Portfolio samples.
- Evaluations or progress records.
- Semiannual progress reports with attendance and individualized assessments.
- Testing documentation.

Claire's note:

If you are using Option 1, do not rely on a general homeschool checklist. Ask your district what they expect to see and keep your records in a way that would make sense to someone outside your house.

### Option 2: SCAIHS

Option 2 runs through the South Carolina Association of Independent Home Schools. The statute gives the broad shape, but SCAIHS gets much more practical about what members need to report or keep.

What to consider keeping:

- SCAIHS membership/enrollment records.
- 180-day attendance record.
- Subject or curriculum plan.
- SCAIHS reports or forms.
- Testing documentation based on your student's grade/department.
- Work samples and progress notes for your own records.
- High school records if applicable.

Claire's note:

If you are an Option 2 family, your next best source is SCAIHS itself. The law gives the outline, but the association tells you what they expect from members this year.

### Option 3: Homeschool Association

Option 3 runs through a homeschool association with at least fifty members. This is the path many South Carolina homeschool families talk about because it gives accountability without the same district-approval structure as Option 1.

What to consider keeping:

- Association membership documentation.
- 180-day attendance record.
- Plan book, diary, or other record of subjects and activities.
- Portfolio samples of student work.
- Semiannual progress reports.
- Progress notes by required subject.

Claire's note:

Once you know your association, recordkeeping gets less foggy. Keep your 180 days, save samples, write down what you covered, and do the progress reports your association expects. You do not need to make it fancy. You need to make it findable.

## Florida

Reviewed on: July 29, 2026

Tier: Portfolio / annual evaluation

### Quick Answer

Florida families have a few separate pieces to keep straight: the Letter of Intent, the portfolio you maintain, the annual evaluation, and the Letter of Termination when the home education program ends.

Official places to verify:

- Florida Department of Education parent resources: https://www.fldoe.org/schools/school-choice/home-edu/parent-resources.stml
- Florida Statute Section 1002.41: https://leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=1000-1099/1002/Sections/1002.41.html

What to consider keeping:

- Copy of your Letter of Intent.
- Portfolio of records and materials.
- Log of educational activities.
- Titles of reading materials used.
- Samples of writings, worksheets, workbooks, or creative materials.
- Dated work samples.
- Annual evaluation documentation.
- Letter of Termination when applicable.
- A simple attendance/calendar record for your own clarity.

Claire's note:

Florida can feel like a lot until you separate the pieces. There is the notice, the portfolio you keep as the year happens, and the annual evaluation you send in. If you keep the portfolio slowly, a few dated samples and notes at a time, the evaluation part feels much less like a scramble.

## New York

Reviewed on: July 29, 2026

Tier: Higher oversight

### Quick Answer

New York is a write-it-down state. Families should verify the current NYSED requirements, but the big recordkeeping pieces usually include written notice, IHIP, quarterly reports, attendance, hours, required subjects, and annual assessment.

Official places to verify:

- NYSED Home Instruction: https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/home-instruction
- NYSED Home Instruction Q&A: https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/home-instruction-questions-and-answers
- 8 NYCRR 100.10: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/8-NYCRR-100.10

What to consider keeping:

- Letter of Intent.
- IHIP.
- Quarterly reports.
- Attendance records.
- Hours by quarter and year.
- Subject/materials covered.
- Grades or narrative evaluations.
- Annual assessment documentation.
- Correspondence with the district or central office.
- Work samples or evidence of progress.

Claire's note:

New York is not the state where I would trust myself to remember everything later. That is not because you are doing anything wrong. It is because the system asks for written proof. Give yourself a steady place for attendance, hours, reports, subjects, and evidence now, before the year gets blurry.

## Pennsylvania

Reviewed on: July 29, 2026

Tier: Portfolio / evaluator review

### Quick Answer

Pennsylvania is a portfolio-and-evaluator state. The public sources describe affidavit or unsworn declaration, education objectives, portfolio records, testing in certain grades, and an annual evaluation report.

Official places to verify:

- Pennsylvania Department of Education home education page: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/home-education-and-private-tutoring
- Pennsylvania Home Education Program BEC: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/policies-acts-and-laws/basic-education-circulars-becs/purdons-statutes/home-education-program

What to consider keeping:

- Affidavit or unsworn declaration.
- Education objectives by subject.
- Immunization/medical or exemption documentation as applicable.
- Attendance days or hours.
- Reading/materials log.
- Work samples.
- Portfolio materials.
- Standardized test results for grades 3, 5, and 8 when applicable.
- Evaluator letter/report.
- District correspondence.
- High school course records when applicable.

Claire's note:

Pennsylvania is not a "wing it in May" state. But the recordkeeping itself can still be simple. Keep the log, save a few good samples over time, know your evaluator's preferences, and do not wait until the end of the year to remember what happened.

## Texas

Reviewed on: July 29, 2026

Tier: Light recordkeeping

### Quick Answer

Texas is lighter from a state-oversight standpoint. TEA says it does not regulate, index, monitor, approve, register, or accredit homeschool programs. But "light regulation" does not mean "keep nothing."

Official places to verify:

- Texas Education Agency home schooling page: https://tea2.tea.texas.gov/families-and-students/finding-school-your-child/home-schooling
- Texas Education Agency alternative schooling page: https://tea.texas.gov/families-and-students/finding-school-your-child/alternative-schooling
- Texas Education Code Section 1.010: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/ED/htm/ED.1.htm

What to consider keeping:

- Written curriculum plan or list.
- Attendance/calendar for your own clarity.
- Work samples.
- Reading/materials log.
- Progress notes.
- High school course records or transcript materials when applicable.
- Copy of any assurance letter if a district requests one.

Claire's note:

Texas may not ask you for much, but future-you still deserves a record. Keep enough that you can see what your child learned, explain your curriculum if needed, and make future transitions easier.
