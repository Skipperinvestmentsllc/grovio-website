## South Carolina

State status: `ready_for_copy`

Reviewed on: 2026-07-29

South Carolina is a multi-pathway state with three homeschool options. The tool must ask families which option they use or plan to use before giving a recordkeeping summary.

### Pathway: Option One, school district

Status: `ready_for_copy`

Tier: Higher oversight

Working summary:

Option One is conducted under the local school district if approved by the district board of trustees. It carries more direct district oversight than the association pathways and includes district-facing approval, record inspection, semiannual reporting, and testing language.

Records to consider keeping:

- District approval/application documentation.
- Parent qualification documentation if requested.
- 180-day attendance record.
- Hours record showing at least 4.5 instructional hours per instructional day, excluding lunch and recess.
- Required subjects plan.
- Plan book, diary, or other written record of subjects and activities.
- Portfolio samples of student academic work.
- Evaluations/progress records.
- Semiannual progress reports with attendance records and individualized assessments.
- Statewide/basic skills testing documentation.

What to verify:

- Your district's current application/approval process.
- District-specific expectations for semiannual reports.
- How testing is administered or arranged.
- Whether the district requests a particular record format.

Claire note draft:

South Carolina Option One is the path where the district is much more directly involved. If this is your route, do not rely on a general homeschool checklist. Read the official source and ask your district what they expect before you build your records.

### Pathway: Option Two, SCAIHS

Status: `ready_for_copy`

Tier: Association oversight

Working summary:

Option Two is conducted under the South Carolina Association of Independent Home Schools. Section 59-65-45 gives minimum standards for SCAIHS: parent qualification, 180 instructional days, and required basic subject areas. It does not spell out the same parent-held record list that Option One and Option Three statutes do. SCAIHS publishes recordkeeping forms/resources and testing requirements, so public copy should tell families to verify current department-level SCAIHS expectations for reporting, testing, transcripts, and records.

Records to consider keeping:

- SCAIHS membership/enrollment documentation.
- Parent qualification documentation if requested.
- 180-day attendance record.
- Subject/curriculum plan covering required basic areas.
- Any SCAIHS reporting forms.
- SCAIHS testing documentation according to the student's department/grade.
- SCAIHS transcript, report card, or progress documentation where applicable.
- Work samples and parent notes for family records.

What to verify:

- Current SCAIHS membership requirements.
- Current SCAIHS reporting deadlines by department/grade.
- Current SCAIHS testing expectations by department/grade.
- What SCAIHS keeps permanently versus what the family should keep at home.
- Whether course, grading, high school, or special needs records have additional expectations.

Claire note draft:

If you are using Option Two, your next best step is to read SCAIHS instructions directly. The statute gives the broad shape, but SCAIHS tells you what they expect from members this year. In association states, the association's rules are not just extra details; they are part of how the pathway works.

### Pathway: Option Three, homeschool association

Status: `ready_for_copy`

Tier: Structured records

Working summary:

Option Three is conducted under a homeschool association with at least fifty members. The statute describes minimum standards: parent qualification, 180 instructional days, required basic subjects, and parent-held educational records. Those records include a plan book/diary or other record, portfolio samples, and semiannual progress reports that include attendance records and individualized progress documentation.

Records to consider keeping:

- 180-day attendance record.
- Plan book, diary, or written record of subjects and activities.
- Portfolio samples of student work.
- Semiannual progress reports.
- Individualized progress notes by required subject.
- Association membership documentation.

What to verify:

- Your specific association's forms and deadlines.
- Whether the association requests periodic or year-end record checks.
- Any association-specific retention period.
- Whether the association has additional expectations beyond the statutory minimums.

Claire note draft:

Option Three is the path many South Carolina homeschool moms hear about first because it runs through an accountability association. Once you know your association, your recordkeeping gets less foggy: keep your 180 days, save samples, write down what you covered, and do the progress reports your association expects.

## Florida

State status: `ready_for_copy`

Reviewed on: 2026-07-29

Tier: Portfolio / annual evaluation

Official sources:

- Florida Department of Education parent home education resources: https://www.fldoe.org/schools/school-choice/home-edu/parent-resources.stml
- Florida Department of Education home education page: https://www.fldoe.org/schools/school-choice/home-edu/
- Florida Statutes Section 1002.41, Home education programs: https://leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&Search_String=&URL=1000-1099/1002/Sections/1002.41.html

Secondary/context sources:

- Homeschooling Florida evaluation overview: https://homeschoolingflorida.com/evaluations/
- U.S. Department of Education Florida state regulation summary: https://www.ed.gov/birth-grade-12-education/education-choice/state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools/florida-state-regulation-of-private-and-home-schools

Working summary:

Florida's official parent resource page describes three statutory parent requirements: file a Letter of Intent, submit annual evaluations, and file a Letter of Termination when the home education program ends. Florida statute also says the parent maintains a portfolio of records and materials, preserves it for 2 years, and makes it available for inspection if requested with 15 days' written notice. Annual evaluation is separate from the portfolio requirement and can be completed through several methods, including a certified-teacher portfolio review, nationally normed test administered by a certified teacher, state assessment, psychologist evaluation, or another mutually agreed valid measurement tool.

Records to consider keeping:

- Copy of Letter of Intent.
- Portfolio of records and materials.
- Contemporaneous log of educational activities.
- Titles of reading materials used.
- Dated work samples.
- Writings, worksheets, workbooks, or creative materials used or developed by the student.
- Attendance/calendar record for parent clarity, even though Florida home education is excluded from school-day requirements.
- Annual evaluation documentation.
- Letter of Termination when applicable.

What to verify:

- District home education contact.
- Annual evaluation options.
- Portfolio expectations for the evaluator if using portfolio evaluation.
- Portfolio retention and inspection language.
- Any local submission process.

Claire note draft:

Florida can feel like a lot until you separate the pieces. There is the notice, the portfolio you keep as the year happens, and the annual evaluation you send in. If you keep the portfolio slowly, a few dated samples and notes at a time, the evaluation part feels much less like a scramble.

## New York

State status: `ready_for_copy`

Reviewed on: 2026-07-29

Tier: Higher oversight

Official sources:

- NYSED Home Instruction landing page: https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/home-instruction
- NYSED Home Instruction Q&A: https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/home-instruction-questions-and-answers
- NYSED key laws and regulations page: https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/key-laws-and-regulations-relating-home-instruction
- New York regulation 8 NYCRR 100.10: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/8-NYCRR-100.10
- NYC home schooling IHIP guidance, useful for NYC-administered families: https://www.schools.nyc.gov/enrollment/enrollment-help/home-schooling/individualized-home-instruction-plan-ihip-grades-9-12

Working summary:

New York has higher documentation expectations. Sources describe written notice of intent, IHIP, quarterly reports, attendance records, required subjects, annual instruction hours, and annual assessment. Requirements vary by grade level and local administration, so public copy must be careful and specific. NYSED's Q&A says parents are required to keep attendance records and recommends keeping evidence of the program, achievement, and correspondence with the district.

Records to consider keeping:

- Letter of Intent.
- IHIP.
- Quarterly reports.
- Attendance records.
- Hours by quarter/year.
- Subject/materials covered.
- Grades or narrative evaluations.
- Annual assessment documentation.
- Correspondence with district or central office.
- Work samples/evidence of progress.
- Required-subject planning by grade level.

Key details to verify:

- Letter of intent timing, including mid-year start timing.
- IHIP submission process and deficiency/revision timelines.
- 180 days.
- 900 annual hours for grades 1-6.
- 990 annual hours for grades 7-12.
- Quarterly report dates.
- Quarterly report contents: hours, material covered, grade or narrative evaluation, and explanation if less than 80 percent of planned material was covered.
- Annual assessment options.
- Required subjects by grade.
- District/NYC-specific submission process.

Claire note draft:

New York is a write-it-down state. That does not mean you are failing before you start. It means your future self needs a steady place for attendance, hours, reports, subjects, and proof, because trying to rebuild it from memory is the part that feels awful.

## Pennsylvania

State status: `ready_for_copy`

Reviewed on: 2026-07-29

Tier: Portfolio / evaluator review

Official sources:

- Pennsylvania Department of Education home education/private tutoring page: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/home-education-and-private-tutoring
- Pennsylvania Department of Education Home Education Program BEC: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/policies-acts-and-laws/basic-education-circulars-becs/purdons-statutes/home-education-program

Secondary/context sources:

- Homeschool Pennsylvania portfolio/logs explanation: https://homeschoolpennsylvania.org/index.php/knowledgebase/portfolios/
- Pennsylvania Home Educators Association evaluation FAQ: https://phea.net/pa-home-education-evaluations/faqs-on-evaluations/

Working summary:

Pennsylvania requires more structured records. PDE describes affidavit or unsworn declaration, proposed education objectives, annual evaluation report by June 30, and testing in grades 3, 5, and 8. Secondary Pennsylvania homeschool sources describe the portfolio as including a contemporaneous reading/materials log, samples of student work, required test results for grades 3, 5, and 8, and verification of either 180 days or the applicable hours requirement. Evaluator preferences may affect how families organize the portfolio, so public copy should tell families to ask the evaluator what they want to see.

Records to consider keeping:

- Affidavit or unsworn declaration.
- Education objectives by subject.
- Immunization/medical or exemption documentation as applicable.
- Attendance days or hours.
- Reading/materials log.
- Work samples.
- Portfolio materials.
- Standardized test results in grades 3, 5, and 8.
- Evaluator letter/report.
- Correspondence with district.
- High school course/credit records when applicable.

What to verify:

- District filing preferences.
- Evaluator qualifications.
- Whether the family is using days or hours.
- Required subjects by level.
- Testing requirements for the child's grade.
- What the evaluator wants to review before the annual evaluation.
- June 30 annual evaluation report submission process.

Claire note draft:

Pennsylvania is not a "wing it in May" state. But the recordkeeping itself can still be simple. Keep the log, save a few good samples over time, know your evaluator's preferences, and do not wait until the end of the year to remember what happened.

## Texas

State status: `ready_for_copy`

Reviewed on: 2026-07-29

Tier: Light recordkeeping

Official sources:

- Texas Education Agency home schooling page: https://tea2.tea.texas.gov/families-and-students/finding-school-your-child/home-schooling
- Texas Education Agency alternative schooling page: https://tea.texas.gov/families-and-students/finding-school-your-child/alternative-schooling
- Texas Education Code Section 1.010, prohibition on regulation of home schools effective September 1, 2025: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/ED/htm/ED.1.htm

Working summary:

Texas is comparatively light from a state oversight perspective. TEA says it does not regulate, index, monitor, approve, register, or accredit homeschool programs. TEA describes home schools as needing to be conducted in a bona fide manner with a written curriculum including reading, spelling, grammar, math, and good citizenship. A district may request a letter of assurance. Texas also has a 2025 statutory provision stating that TEA, the State Board of Education, or another educational institution described by statute may not adopt a rule or policy regulating a home school educational program.

Records to consider keeping:

- Written curriculum plan or list.
- Attendance/calendar for parent clarity.
- Work samples.
- Reading/materials log.
- Progress notes.
- High school course records/transcript materials when applicable.
- Copy of any assurance letter if requested.

What to verify:

- Current TEA guidance.
- Whether a district has requested a letter of assurance.
- Transfer-back-to-public-school documentation expectations.
- High school/college documentation needs.

Claire note draft:

Texas may not ask you for much, but that does not mean your year should disappear. Keep enough that you can see what your child learned, explain your curriculum if needed, and make future transitions easier.
